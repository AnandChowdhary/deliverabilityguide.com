# Interview the guide's author

Use this workflow when the author asks to go through sections/proposals and add his wisdom. Read the relevant guide section, the current proposal, and the [decision record](../write-outbound-guide/references/author-decisions.md). Do not restart already-completed interviews or ask him to confirm established preferences again.

## Conversation pattern

Give a short TL;DR of what the guide says or what the proposed addition would say. Ask one focused question about the missing judgment, exception, or practical experience. Let the author answer before moving to the next proposal. Avoid a questionnaire covering the whole book at once.

After an answer, briefly state the decision in his terms, preserving conditions and exceptions, then introduce the next topic. “All good” approves the presented proposal. A short answer such as “Judgment” is a decision; it is not an invitation to invent a numerical rule or keep pressing for one.

Capture relationships precisely. Examples from this interview:

- A single interim update requires **both** a major development the prospect cares about and a business-related delay while they are working. Leave means no interim nudges.
- An OOO message listing an alternate does not mean the colleague personally introduced you.
- One engaged contact does not imply an account-wide pause. The author's preference is multiple coordinated relationships, brought together near buying readiness.
- “Automatic negative handling” may mean stopping contact, not sending another AI email.

If a statement is ambiguous enough to change the advice, ask a narrow clarification. Do not turn obvious shorthand into a prolonged interrogation. Flag a conflict with an important provider rule separately and show the proposed adjustment, rather than silently rewriting the author's strategy.

## Incorporation and continuity

When the author wants the whole discussion completed before incorporation, keep the published text unchanged during that interview. Save accepted notes and pending topics in tracked decision context as appropriate so an interrupted/new session can continue. Use `.context/` for scratch research, not as the only durable record.

Once the planned proposals are answered, incorporate the accepted advice without asking for the same approval again. Use the writing workflow to update relevant sections, eliminate contradictions, and refresh TL;DR summaries. Mark discussed topics as incorporated/current; do not leave accepted edits labeled as pending.

Keep the final progress report concrete: what changed, what remains unresolved if anything, and whether it has been validated and published. The author's request to approve direction changes is not a reason to stop routine approved editing or release work.
